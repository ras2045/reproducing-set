Ten of Eighteen

Look at the gap between one prime and the next, and take that gap mod 18. There are 18 possible remainders. Only 10 of them ever happen — not approximately, not usually. Never the other 8.

The claim

Take every pair of consecutive primes (p, q) and compute (q − p) mod 18. Across every prime up to 300,000,000 — 16,252,325 of them — that value only ever lands on one of ten residues: 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16. The other eight, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17, never occur. Zero counterexamples at that scale, and the reason isn't statistical — it's forced.

The mechanism, worked in full

Every prime past 2 is odd. So for any two consecutive primes past the very start of the list, both are odd — and odd minus odd is always even. That alone rules out every odd residue mod 18 except one: whatever gap involves the single even prime, 2. The only such gap is the first one, 2 → 3, which equals 1. Every other gap in existence is even, so its residue mod 18 must be one of the nine even values. Nine even residues, plus that one forced exception, is ten. There is no eleventh case to find — the mechanism is exhaustive by parity alone.

A correction, on the record

An earlier phrasing of this result described the ten residues as "coprime to 18," which happens to be numerically true for most of them but implies extra structure — some mod-3 relationship — that isn't actually doing any work. The real mechanism is parity alone: even-gap forced, plus one odd exception. The stronger-sounding phrasing was replaced with the precise one. Getting caught overclaiming your own result and fixing it in public is part of what a lab notebook is for.
