The Big Picture

Of the small things that make a strong house. A practical guide to framing methods that produce a genuinely sound structure — not facades, finishes, or curb appeal.

The argument

Stand in a finished house and you're looking at almost none of what makes it strong. The drywall carries no load. The paint carries no load. By the time finish work starts, the house's real strength was already decided — in framing, a stage most people never examine closely. A house doesn't fail because someone forgot to build a foundation. It fails from an accumulation of small framing decisions, each individually defensible, collectively wrong. Get enough of them right and the house is strong in a way that has nothing to do with how it looks.

The maxim

“Level the plate; do not survey the errors.”

Given an imperfection in what you've been handed, there are two moves. Describe it — measure it, note it, carry it forward as a correction to remember later. Or absorb it — find one true reference and bring everything else to meet it, once, so the imperfection stops existing for every measurement after. A leveled plate removes a whole class of future measurement from the job; a survey just adds another row to a table someone has to keep consulting forever. Every technique in this book, from a foundation to a roof to a run of stairs, is one instruction applied at a different scale: does this method describe the error, or does it absorb it?
